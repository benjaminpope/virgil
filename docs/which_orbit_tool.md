# Which orbit tool?

!!! warning "Provisional"
    May change in any 0.4.x release without a deprecation cycle. `virgil.orbit_search` is provisional in the whole; `virgil.epochs` and `virgil.aliases` too (see [stability](stability.md)).

Most data need only the first row; the rest of this page is for when you need one of the tools it calls yourself.

virgil has these tools to find a binary's orbit in interferometric data from several epochs. They answer different questions and take the data in different forms. All need the `orbits` extra (`pip install "virgil-astro[orbits]"`), which provides jaxoplanet to solve Kepler's equation. All take an [`Epochs`][virgil.epochs.Epochs] (one named snapshot per dataset), except `fit_orbit_aliases`, which takes a list of [`OIData`][virgil.oidata.OIData], one per epoch, with per-exposure times.

| You want | Use | Goes in | Comes out | Use it when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A blind orbit from OIFITS files, end to end | [`OrbitPipeline`](pipeline.md#orbitpipeline-a-binary-seen-on-several-nights-provisional) | OIFITS files, an `Epochs`, or `{night: data}` | A run folder and a `Result` | Most data; the [orbit tutorial](orbit_fitting.md) leads with it |
| A quick ranking of trial orbits, or a start for a fit of a model of your own | [`rank_orbits`][virgil.epochs.rank_orbits], or [`start_from_positions`][virgil.epochs.start_from_positions] (superseded by `fit_orbit_aliases` and `OrbitPipeline` when the period is unknown) to rank and fit in one call | `rank_orbits`: `Epochs`, a function from an orbit to a scene, and the trial orbits (for example from [`starting_orbits`][virgil.orbits.starting_orbits]). `start_from_positions` builds its own trial orbits: it takes `Epochs`, a scene function of the sampled parameters, `priors`, `start_values(orbit, flux)`, a position `grid`, a `periods` range and `t_ref` | [`RankedOrbits`][virgil.epochs.RankedOrbits]: orbits best first with their scale-marginalized log likelihoods; `start_from_positions` returns an [`OrbitStart`][virgil.epochs.OrbitStart] with the ranked candidates and fits from the best distinct ones | You have orbits to compare, or a fit that needs a start in the right basin |
| Scores with a flux shared across epochs, with radial-velocity or position terms | [`score_orbits`][virgil.orbit_search.score_orbits] (provisional) | a function `model(orbit, flux)` returning a scene, `Epochs`, candidate orbits, and optionally [`RVData`][virgil.orbits.RVData] or [`PositionData`][virgil.orbits.PositionData] terms | [`OrbitScores`][virgil.orbit_search.OrbitScores]: one score per candidate with the companion's flux integrated out, the profiled flux and its error, and flags; rank them with `OrbitScores.order` | The candidates are many, the flux is the same on every night, or other measurements of the orbit exist |
| The period aliases of an undersampled orbit, with evidences | [`fit_orbit_aliases`][virgil.aliases.fit_orbit_aliases] | A list of `OIData`, one per epoch, optionally the [`epoch_positions`][virgil.epochs.epoch_positions] of the same data, and a period range (by default taken from the data) | [`AliasResult`][virgil.aliases.AliasResult]: a band for each integer number of orbits between epochs with its evidence and probability, the best orbit, and posterior samples; `AliasResult.save` and `load` keep it | The epochs are further apart than the period, so that more than one period fits the positions |
| One orbit, and its posterior | [`fit`][virgil.fitting.fit] with a [`KeplerOrbit`][virgil.orbits.KeplerOrbit] scene | `epochs.model_fn(scene_fn)`, where `scene_fn` maps the sampled parameters to an [`OrbitalBinary`][virgil.models.OrbitalBinary]; priors; and `epochs.data` | [`FitResult`][virgil.fitting.FitResult]: the maximum a posteriori orbit; sample the posterior with `numpyro_model` and NUTS | You know which period alias is the right one, or the orbit is well sampled |

## How to choose

1. **The sampling constrains the period well.** Run `OrbitPipeline`, which does the next steps for you ([Orbits from interferometric data](orbit_fitting.md)). By hand: start from per-night positions ([`epoch_positions`][virgil.epochs.epoch_positions]), rank trial orbits with `rank_orbits` (`start_from_positions` is superseded by `fit_orbit_aliases`, which also weighs the aliases), then fit and sample the best orbit with `fit`.
2. **You have many candidates, a flux shared by all epochs to marginalize over, or radial velocities or astrometry too.** Score the candidates with `score_orbits`, which adds those terms, then fit the best.
3. **The epochs undersample the period** (the gaps are longer than the period). Run `fit_orbit_aliases`, which fits each alias band to the observables of all epochs and gives its probability. Flagged nights (an alias peak, a separation below the resolution) seed nothing but still count in the likelihood. Read `AliasResult.period_bound` and `period_at_lower_bound` before quoting a period ([how the window is set](orbit_methods.md#alias-search)).
4. **Then fit the winner.** The best orbit of an alias band starts `fit` with a `KeplerOrbit`; its posterior samples come with the result.

The scale marginalization of `rank_orbits`, `start_from_positions` and `score_orbits`, their model shapes and argument order are in [scoring options](orbit_methods.md#scoring-options).

Angle and sign conventions are on the [conventions](conventions.md#orbits) page, and the helpers behind these tools in [orbit methods](orbit_methods.md).
